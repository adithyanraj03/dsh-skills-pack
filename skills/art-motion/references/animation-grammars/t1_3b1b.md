# t1_3b1b · 3Blue1Brown-style math / concept visualization

> One-line grammar: **on a black background put only "the one thing being explained right now", use smooth Transform to turn the previous concept straight into the next one, color = the concept's name, and the film almost never cuts.**
>
> Demo film: `scripts/engine/demos/t1_3b1b/` (render `render.py --film demos/t1_3b1b`) (8s, 3 shots) · overview `assets/animation-grammars/t1_3b1b_overview.jpg` · code `scripts/engine/demos/t1_3b1b/`

## "Before you start" checklist

1. **For every shot, write down "who becomes whom".** What the previous object is and what the next object is (grid → circle, one curve → another, pixel → neuron). If you cannot write the sentence naming the morphing object, do not use t1 — use y5 or y3 instead.
2. **The first frame is the object.** Axes, grid and curve come out first, title and formula arrive later; do not Write a title line at second 0.
3. **Count the words on screen.** Never more than 2 points on screen at the same moment; before the third appears, the earlier ones either become a graphic or are taken away. Writing points one by one as a list is just a black-background slide deck.
4. **Write the color legend first.** One color per concept; the symbols in the formula and the objects on screen share the same color; no more than 4 semantic colors in the whole film.
5. **Stay still during wait.** No constant slow push, no breathing, no floating; the camera moves only when you need to look at the next object, and it stops when it arrives (see ③ "Camera").

## ① When to use it

- **Use it**: to explain how one mechanism "turns into another thing step by step" — neural network forward propagation, attention, matrix transformation, gradient descent, probability distributions, algorithm flow. Best when the VO is made of **action sentences** like "we flatten this image into a column" or "each pixel is multiplied by a weight": every sentence maps to one Transform.
- **Use it**: when the audience needs to "see the math" — the symbols in the formula map one-to-one to objects on screen (the W in the formula is blue, and so are the connecting lines).
- **Do not use it**: product launches, mood/brand films (black background with white lines is too cold, too slow); real data trends (that is t3's job); when the VO is opinions, story or quote lines (there is no object that can "morph", and forcing it leaves only decoration). Fast cuts under 8 seconds do not fit either — 3b1b's native rhythm is one animation of 1–3 seconds plus a 1-second wait (see ⑤); compressing that into 8 seconds and 3 shots is already the limit.

## ③ Motion

- **Easing**: default `smooth = 6t⁵−15t⁴+10t³` (the 3b1b version, velocity and acceleration both zero at both ends; the CE version is a sigmoid, `MO.smoothCE`). **Almost every animation uses it**; linear is only for Write, constant-speed rotation and the advance of ShowPassingFlash. `there_and_back` for Indicate, `rush_into/rush_from` for "rush in/rush out".
- **Default durations**: Animation run_time=1s, wait=1s. Source statistics from 2023–24: the most common explicit run_time is 2s (481 times) > 3s (333) > 1s (187) > 4s (145).
- **LaggedStart**: default lag_ratio 0.05; the most used in the source is 0.1. Implementation: `MO.lagged(i, n, p, r)` (item i occupies `[i·r, i·r+1]`, total length `1+(n−1)r`). This film uses 0.01 for the 100 pixels→neurons (done in 1.1s) and 0.006 for the 160 edges.
- **Write**: linear; 1s for <15 submobjects, otherwise 2s; lag between submobjects = `min(4/(n+1), 0.2)`; each glyph "is outlined first, filled afterwards" (`DG.math`/`DG.write`: lineDash advances the stroke → in the last 60% the fill fades in and the stroke fades out).
- **Transform**: both shapes are resampled to the same number of points and **start-aligned** (both starting from the upper-left direction, clockwise), then point-by-point linear interpolation (path_arc=0) plus color interpolation. `DG.rectPts/circlePts/morph`. This film: grid→circle (pixel→neuron), weight grid→flying to one neuron (weighted sum).
- **Entrance/exit**: FadeIn/FadeOut with shift (this film 24–40px, "from bottom up" or "fading away upward"); new-layer neurons fade in from 24px to the right; anything replaced shrinks to a dot and merges into the "⋮".
- **Signals**: ShowPassingFlash (time_width 2) — duplicate the edge, tint it yellow, width ×1.5, visible segment `[max(0,u−2), min(1,u)]`, u from 0 to 3 (`DG.flash`), staggered by input row with lag 0.04.
- **Camera**: MovingCameraScene frame animation; the camera move is an event: hold → push to the next object to look at → hold. By default push once with 3b1b's native smooth (about 1s, push/to in `CAM.track`); use a fast push only on the beat where the conclusion lands (0.28s pushing in 18–32%, an accent, once or twice per section); 3D scene orbit at 0.5–3°/s (3b1b default 1°/s) only when explaining spatial relationships. In this film shot 2's push toward the output layer (1→1.08) is an event push; shot 1's constant slow push around the grid from 1→1.025 is the old "slow push in every shot" habit — do not copy it in new films.
- **Still during wait**: the originals are usually **still** during wait — this is the biggest difference between 3b1b and launch films. Do not add constant slow push, breathing or floating to manufacture motion; the sense of motion comes from the next Transform.

## ④ Transitions (the 4 most used)

1. **Transform continuation (no cut)**: the last frame of the previous shot = the first frame of the next shot, and morphing turns the old object into the new one. In this film shot 1→2: 100 pixel squares become a column of input neurons, and shot 2's first frame is pixel-identical to shot 1's last frame. This is 3b1b's number-one transition and accounts for the vast majority.
2. **Zoom through**: the camera pushes into an object exponentially (`zoom = Z^e`, constant perceived speed), the object's rim opens into an aperture, and the new image is inside. `TRANSITIONS.zoomThrough`, parameters `live:true` (the old shot keeps pushing the camera itself while lt>duration, redrawn as vectors so it stays sharp), `r0` (object radius), `zoom ≥ 1100/r0` (otherwise the aperture does not cover the screen and the last frame jumps), `focus` (the new image's subject sits in the aperture center first, and only returns to place at e>0.55). This film shot 2→3: first tint the stroke of the hidden neuron with activation 0.95 yellow ("let's look at this one"), then push into it 48×, 0.75s.
3. **FadeOut(shift=UP) + FadeIn(shift=UP)**: when changing topic everything fades away upward and the new content fades in from below (`TRANSITIONS.fadeShift`).
4. **ReplacementTransform for title/formula**: symbols of the old formula move to the position of the same-named symbol in the new formula (TransformMatchingTex, default 2s in the 3b1b version); symbols that do not match fade out and in.

## ⑤ Pacing and text

- **Shot duration**: the originals are continuous scenes, a single animation of 1–3s followed by a 1s wait; one "shot" (one concept) often runs 8–20s. This film's 8 seconds and 3 shots (2.6/2.8/2.6s) is a **compressed version**; used in real VO work it should be stretched 2–3×.
- **Alignment with VO**: Grant's process is **lay out the animations first, record the VO afterwards** ("I start by laying out all of the animations… then once I record the voiceover, the actual editing is like a day"). So the alignment rule is "one sentence = one animation": the animation starts when the sentence reaches the action verb, and after the sentence settles there is a wait of about 1s to let the audience look.
- **Text**: title/question is Written before the action (ask first, then perform); formulas appear together with the image and in the same color; the value (0.95) counts along with the fill and never appears before the image. In Chinese, no more than one title line plus one annotation line per screen.
- **FAQ principle**: "Every movement on the screen should be deliberate, with an identifiable purpose" — any motion without an explanatory purpose must be deleted.

## ⑧ The 3 most common mistakes

1. **Treating "glow" as 3b1b's signature and adding bloom plus a dark-blue gradient background.** The originals are pure black plus highly saturated thin lines plus ×1.5 saturation, with almost no halo. Add a halo and it becomes a "techy slide deck".
2. **Using linear or ordinary ease everywhere, with too many fast animations.** The default must be smooth (quintic polynomial), one animation of 1–3s followed by a 1s hold; every motion must correspond to a verb in the VO.
3. **Changing the color coding mid-film / too many colors.** One color = one concept, the symbols in the formula and the objects on screen share the same color; past 3–4 semantic colors the audience cannot keep track.
